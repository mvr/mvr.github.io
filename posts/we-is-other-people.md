---
title: "\"We\" is Other People"
published: August 5, 2026
tags: pamphleteering
---

There's a pattern I've seen more and more in political commentary,
especially the Australian sources I pay attention to, where someone
will make a sweeping diagnosis of a social problem in the language of
things that *we* do wrong. <!--more-->

* *We* are trapped in social media echo chambers.
* *We* are obsessed with the culture wars.
* *We* are becoming more and more polarised.
* *We* are distrustful of experts.
* *We* are motivated by grievance.
* *We* are uncomfortable with complexity.
* *We* are falling for foreign propaganda.
* *We* are censorious of ideas we don't like.
* *We* are always looking for a scapegoat.
* *We* are allowing billionaires to pit us against one another.
* *We* are too focused on the short term.
* ...

But when it comes time for the speaker to supply examples, every
single one has the same political valence; the opposite of the one
held by the speaker.

The statements themselves often have a kernel of truth, and are worth
properly analysing and discussing. Taken earnestly, each could be a
little piece of self-criticism, and you could imagine the speaker
giving an example of a time *they* realised they were stuck in an echo
chamber, baited by the culture wars ... Of course this doesn't ever
happen. The purpose of making the claim is not analysis or discussion,
but point-scoring: using these claims as a stick to beat the people
you were planning to beat anyway.

The straw that broke the camel's back for me was an
[episode](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/angertainment/id1459965243?i=1000775751144)
of the [Democracy
Sausage](https://ausi.anu.edu.au/democracy-sausage-mark-kenny)
podcast, where between them the guest and hosts managed to hit almost
everything on the list above. I don't mean to pick on this podcast
specifically; it's just that once you are prepared for it, you
see the pattern everywhere. 

I could continue to name and shame, but here's something more
constructive^[Though I'm about to use the law of excluded middle, har
har.]. Keep an ear out for this pattern, and when you come across it,
try hard to think of additional examples that go the other way. One of
two things can happen. If you come up with some, great; but this means
the speaker has missed an opportunity to properly come to terms with
this real issue they have identified. If you can't, also great; but
this means you (and likely the speaker) don't believe *we* are all
guilty of the fault. Podcasters should just be honest when they feel
the same way.
